INTERACTIVE TEACHING-LEARNING RESOURCE

Projectile Motion — Interactive Scratch Model

Subject: Physics Topic: Projectile Motion Platform: Scratch Category: Interactive Teaching-Learning Resource

Project Gallery

Scratch IDE – Projectile Motion Code

Project Overview

This interactive learning model was developed using Scratch to help students understand the concept of Projectile Motion through visual representation and exploration. It demonstrates how an object moves when projected at an angle under the influence of gravity, following a curved (parabolic) trajectory.

The model helps students understand important concepts such as horizontal and vertical components of velocity, the effect of gravity, maximum height, time of flight, and horizontal range. Instead of learning these concepts only through mathematical formulas, students can observe the motion visually, making the learning process more engaging, interactive, and easier to understand.

Key Concepts Visualized

Horizontal Velocity Component (v_x = v cos θ) Vertical Velocity Component (v_y = v sin θ - gt) Parabolic Trajectory Path Maximum Height (H_max) Time of Flight (T) Horizontal Range (R) Effect of Gravity (g = 9.8 m/s²)

Learning Outcomes

  • Understand the concept of projectile motion.
  • Identify horizontal and vertical components of velocity.
  • Visualize the parabolic path of a projectile.
  • Understand the role of gravity in projectile motion.
  • Connect theoretical physics concepts with visual demonstrations.

HANDS-ON PHYSICS MODEL • TLR

GRAVITRON – Explore Gravity, Balance & Stability

Subject: Physics Topics: Centre of Gravity, Balance and Stability Category: Teaching-Learning Resource Project Type: Hands-on Interactive Physics Model

Project Overview

GRAVITRON is a hands-on physics learning model designed to help students understand the concepts of balance, centre of gravity, and stability through practical exploration.

The model consists of a balancing beam with movable weights that students can adjust at different positions to observe how the distribution of weight affects balance. By changing the position of the weights, students can investigate why an object remains balanced in some situations but becomes unstable in others. This interactive approach encourages students to predict, experiment, observe, and understand the underlying physics principles rather than relying only on theoretical explanations.

Core Physics Concepts

Centre of Gravity (CoG) Conditions for Balance Weight Distribution & Stability Torque & Moments Predict → Experiment → Observe

Learning Outcomes

  • Understand the concept of centre of gravity.
  • Explore the conditions required for balance.
  • Investigate how weight distribution affects stability.
  • Observe the effect of changing the position of weights.
  • Develop experimental and problem-solving skills.

PHYSICS EDUCATION • VISUAL ANIMATION

Average Speed – Interactive Visual Animation

Subject: Physics Topic: Average Speed and Instantaneous Speed Category: Physics Education Project Type: Visual Learning Animation

Project Overview

This project is a visual animation developed to help students understand the concept of Average Speed in Physics. It demonstrates the motion of a car travelling between different points over specific time intervals.

Through this animation, students can observe how distance and time are related and calculate average speed for different parts of the journey as well as the complete journey. It also helps learners distinguish between average speed and instantaneous speed through visual representation. The animation makes the concept easier to understand by connecting theoretical formulas with a real-life example of moving objects.

Core Kinematics Principles

Average Speed: v_avg = Total Distance / Total Time Instantaneous Speed: v = ds / dt Distance-Time Relationship (s-t Graph) Secant Line vs. Tangent Slope Multi-Segment Journey Calculations

Learning Outcomes

  • Understand the concept of average speed.
  • Calculate average speed using distance and time.
  • Compare average speeds over different time intervals.
  • Differentiate between average speed and instantaneous speed.
  • Relate motion to its visual representation.

PHYSICS EDUCATION • HANDS-ON STEM

Magnus Effect Teaching Activity

Subject: Physics Topic: Aerodynamics & Bernoulli's Principle Category: Physics Education Activity Method: Experiential Demonstration

Project Overview

This experiential activity connects fluid dynamics and Bernoulli's theorem to observable, real-world phenomena. By spinning cylinders and observing the curved flight paths caused by aerodynamic lift forces, students discover how pressure differentials arise in moving fluids.

The activity links classroom physics with sports applications (such as curving a soccer ball or baseball pitch) to generate genuine enthusiasm and deeper conceptual appreciation.

Core Physical Principles

Bernoulli's Principle (P + 0.5ρv² = const) Lift Force Generation Streamline Airflow Asymmetry Rotational Spin Dynamics

Learning Outcomes

  • Understand the physical mechanism generating the Magnus effect.
  • Observe how rotational velocity influences airflow and deflection trajectory.
  • Connect theoretical fluid dynamics with hands-on experiential demonstrations.
